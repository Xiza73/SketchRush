# 01 · Concept

## In one sentence

A drawing-and-guessing game where the clock decides: you score for how much time
was left when you named what somebody drew, and the person drawing scores for how
fast the room understood them.

## The idea

- One player draws, everybody else guesses. Turns rotate so everyone draws.
- The drawer picks one of three words and has a fixed time to get it across.
- Every guesser has the same clock. Naming it early is worth far more than
  naming it late.
- The drawer's score **is** the room's score: the faster and the more people who
  get it, the more they earn.
- Rooms of 2 to 10, words in Spanish or English, emote reactions, and an
  accumulated table across rounds.

## What makes it different

1. **The clock rules.** Points are the percentage of the turn still on the clock
   when you guess — the same idea that drives [WordRush](../../../WordRush), and
   the reason both games feel like the same product.
2. **Drawing well is the only way to score while drawing.** The drawer is paid
   the room's average. Choosing something nobody can guess is not clever; it is
   a zero.
3. **The room decides how people talk.** Guessing runs either through a private
   box — you see your own attempts, the room only sees who got it — or as an
   open chat. It is a room setting, not a house rule.

## Who it is for

The same group that plays WordRush: friends who want a short, noisy game with no
account and no download. It seats more people than WordRush does, because a
drawing game gets better with a crowd and a word game does not.

## Name

Working name: **SketchRush**. It is a placeholder, not decided — `Rush` is there
to mark the family, and the family name is not settled either.
